# Section 2: Corrected finite-dimensional foundations

The original file was a placeholder. The cited Theorem 2.4, Definition 2.5 and
Corollary 3.1 were not stated or proved. Those references are withdrawn.

For a linear map D:V→W of finite inner-product spaces the two-term complex has
H⁰=ker D, H¹=coker D. Adopt Det H=det H⁰⊗(det H¹)*; some authors use the dual.
The determinant functor identifies this line with det V⊗(det W)*. This is a
line, not a specified connection, scalar phase, or physical classification.
An arbitrary two-term complex is not a specified mapping cone of a chain map.
An affine map is not a differential in a cochain complex.

An SVD proves that D*D and DD* share positive spectra with multiplicity.
Their zero multiplicities are dim V−rank D and dim W−rank D. For square D the
index remains zero even when kernels appear. Metrics affect adjoints and spectra.

In finite dimension ζΔ(s)=Σ_{λ>0}λ^(−s) is entire and −ζΔ'(0)=Σlog λ=log det' Δ.
For invertible Δ this is the ordinary determinant. Omitting zeros at a singular
point is not taking a continuous limit. The empty product is one. No UV cutoff,
heat subtraction or analytic continuation is needed. A cutoff changes the
quantity. Infinite-dimensional zeta regularization requires a specified domain,
operator class, continuation regular at zero, scale and normalization; none is
provided here for NS. For example log detζ(Δ/c)=−ζΔ'(0)−log(c)ζΔ(0).

For invertible C¹ square D(t) in dimension n with fixed metrics, Jacobi's formula
proves R'=2 Re tr(D⁻¹D') for R=log det(D*D). If σ_min(D)≥s>0 throughout a path,
|R(b)−R(a)|≤2n∫||D'||₂/s. Alternatively Δ≥gI gives a bound n∫||Δ'||₂/g.
These are derivative/gap bounds, not curvature-only bounds. D(t)=[exp(t)] has
R=2t while every curvature two-form on its parameter interval vanishes, disproving
the previously asserted general curvature-only inequality for R.

For a specified unitary line connection ∇=d+A, Hol(∂Σ)=exp(−∫ΣF) on a spanning
surface with the appropriate trivialization. Angular distance to one is bounded
by ∫Σ|F|. Open-path scalars require endpoint frames and logarithms require branch
choices. A discrete set of seeds is not a smooth surface parameter. A local
system is flat; calling its curvature nonzero is inconsistent.

Differentiating a smooth orthogonal projector P²=P gives PP'P=0 and tr(PP')=0.
Thus the former formula cannot be a general Berry/Quillen connection. In a local
orthonormal frame Q the projected connection is Q*dQ, with determinant form
tr(Q*dQ). Rank jumps prevent such a constant-rank harmonic frame. Acyclicity does
not force every geometric connection on the determinant line to be flat.

Spectral flow here counts negative-to-positive zero crossings of a self-adjoint
path positively, with invertible endpoints. In finite dimension it equals
n_minus(start)−n_minus(end); regular crossing forms determine local signs.
D*D≥0 has no signed negative-to-positive crossing. A general evolution matrix is
not self-adjoint. The dilation [[0,D*],[D,0]] has paired ± singular values and net
SF zero for invertible endpoints. There is no universal formula Hol=exp(iπ SF)
for these matrix families. Bismut–Freed/eta-invariant theorems require geometric
analytic hypotheses and conventions not constructed here. Winding, SF and
open-path transport must not be conflated. No program-equivalence or
zero-knowledge theorem follows from the estimates above.
